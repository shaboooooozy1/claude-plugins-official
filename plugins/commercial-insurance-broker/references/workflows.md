# Workflow Checklists

Follow the matching checklist. Stop at blocking gaps before drafting outbound
client or market documents.

## intake

Goal: decide whether to proceed and what to collect.

1. Capture named insured legal name, DBA, FEIN if provided, years in business,
   and operations in plain language.
2. Map operations to likely ISO/NCCI class codes only as hypotheses.
3. List lines to consider from `coverage-lines.md`.
4. Collect the data set in `acord-and-data.md` for those lines.
5. Identify loss-sensitive items: prior claims, cancelled/non-renewed coverage,
   bankruptcies, uninsured periods, subcontractors, and high-hazard work.
6. Draft an intake memo: account story, target effective date, lines, markets
   likely to fit, and a missing-information list ordered by blocking vs later.

## submission

Goal: assemble a marketable underwriting package.

1. Confirm target effective date, agency/wholesale path, and admitted vs
   surplus-lines posture.
2. Build a market list with reason-for-market notes (appetite, incumbent,
   program, surplus).
3. Assemble submission contents: ACORD apps, loss runs (3–5 years), currently
   valued, financials if needed, SOV, driver schedule, vehicle schedule,
   contract insurance exhibits, safety program, and resume of principals.
4. Draft an underwriter summary: operations, exposures, controls, losses, and
   the specific coverage intent (limits, additional insured, waiver, primary
   and noncontributory).
5. List subjectivities expected up front rather than at bind.

## quotes

Goal: compare apples-to-apples and recommend.

1. Put each quote on one matrix: carrier, rating (AM Best if stated), admitted
   status, limits, deductibles/SIR, form type (occurrence vs claims-made),
   retro date, key exclusions, endorsements, premium, and commission if known.
2. Call out coverage gaps versus the intake intent and versus the competitor.
3. Flag quotes that expire soon, are indications only, or depend on
   unpublished forms.
4. Draft a client-facing comparison with a clear producer recommendation and
   remaining questions.

## bind

Goal: convert a selected quote into a clean bind order.

1. Restate the selected option: carrier, premium, payment plan, effective
   date, and form basis.
2. List every subjectivity and who owns the document.
3. Confirm named insured spelling, additional named insureds, locations,
   vehicles, and mortgagee/loss payee.
4. Draft a bind-order email plus a post-bind checklist: binder, invoice,
   finance agreement, surplus-lines filing if applicable, and diary for
   policy issuance.

## policy-check

Goal: compare issued policy to the bound quote.

1. Check named insured, mailing/physical addresses, policy period, and
   retro date.
2. Check limits, deductibles, coverage parts, and schedule of locations or
   vehicles.
3. Check endorsements against the quote and binder, including additional
   insured, waiver, primary/noncontributory, and manuscript forms.
4. List discrepancies as `critical` (coverage/period/limit) or `clerical`.
5. Draft a carrier correction request for critical items and a client
   delivery note for what is correct.

## coi

Goal: draft certificate instructions that match the policy.

1. Collect holder name, address, project or contract, and requested wording.
2. Map each request to a policy form that must already exist (AI, waiver,
   PNC, notice of cancellation).
3. Refuse to draft wording that the policy cannot support; list the
   endorsement that would be required first.
4. Produce a certificate worksheet: lines, limits, policy numbers if stated,
   description of operations, and special wording to verify.

## endorsement

Goal: place a mid-term change without silent coverage gaps.

1. State the change, requested effective date, and why.
2. Identify affected lines and whether additional premium, underwriting, or
   inspections are needed.
3. List documents required (updated SOV, vehicle ID, contract, loss notice).
4. Draft the carrier/wholesale request and a client confirmation of what is
   not covered until the carrier agrees.

## renewal

Goal: retain, remarket, or restructure before expiration.

1. Diary dates: 120/90/60/30 days, current expiration, and claim-reporting
   windows on claims-made lines.
2. Refresh exposure data and currently valued loss runs.
3. Decide retain vs remarket using losses, rate change, coverage gaps, and
   market appetite.
4. Draft a stewardship summary: three-year premium/loss, open claims, and
   recommended program changes.

## claims

Goal: notice the claim and preserve coverage.

1. Capture date of loss, date the claim was first made against the insured,
   location, parties, injuries/property, and how the producer learned of it.
2. Identify policies that may respond, including expired occurrence policies
   and current and prior claims-made policies. For claims-made lines, check
   the retroactive date of each policy and any extended reporting period, and
   notice the policy in force when the claim was first made (or the ERP that
   applies) rather than defaulting to the current policy.
3. Draft FNOL content for the carrier and a client checklist (do not admit
   liability, preserve evidence, send contracts and incident reports).
4. List coverage questions without deciding coverage: late notice, expected
   or intended, workers vs GL, additional insured status, and SIR obligations.
